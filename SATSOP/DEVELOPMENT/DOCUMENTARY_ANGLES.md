# SATSOP — DOCUMENTARY ANGLES

Updated: 2026-09-24
Status: **working directions; no angle locked**

## Recommended angle — 74 Percent Complete Was Still Not Enough

Central question:

> How can a nuclear plant be nearly three-quarters built and still be cheaper to abandon?

Why it works:

- begins with a visually undeniable mystery;
- converts scale into an economic question;
- provides a clean midpoint reversal when the audience learns that the two Satsop units did not share one ending;
- supports a decision climax using BPA's real 1994 comparison;
- ends with reuse rather than empty ruin imagery.

Story spine:

1. The towers look like the remains of one plant.
2. They are evidence of two projects and two financing systems.
3. Forecast demand made five reactors appear rational.
4. WPPSS expanded faster than its financing and oversight systems could comfortably support.
5. WNP-5 hit the financing wall in 1982.
6. WNP-3 survived because its cost structure was different.
7. By 1994, “already spent” could not outweigh the risk of spending more.
8. The site retained value after the nuclear plan did not.

## Alternative A — The Two Reactors That Failed Differently

Central question:

> Why did two unfinished reactors standing side by side die twelve years apart?

Strength: maximizes the WNP-3/WNP-5 distinction and makes financial structure understandable.
Risk: title may be less immediately visual unless the thumbnail clearly shows both towers.

## Alternative B — The Nuclear Plant That Became an Industrial Park

Central question:

> What can a community do with a megaproject that will never perform its intended job?

Strength: distinctive second-life ending, present-day access, less repetitive than a collapse-only documentary.
Risk: can become a municipal redevelopment profile unless the WPPSS stakes remain dominant.

## Rejected simplifications

- “The largest bond default built these towers.” The default directly concerns Projects 4 and 5, while the most complete Satsop structures belong to WNP-3's separate history.
- “America abandoned a finished nuclear plant.” WNP-3 was incomplete and never operated.
- “One bad forecast caused everything.” Forecast error, financing capacity, construction management, regulation, market alternatives, and sunk-cost decisions require separate evidence.
- “Nature reclaimed the site.” The site has active industrial and training uses.
- “Nuclear fear killed Satsop.” Current evidence does not support a single-cause cultural thesis.

## Tone

Measured, investigative, and visually concrete. The film should explain how reasonable-looking decisions compound into an irrational portfolio without assigning motives unsupported by evidence.
