# SATSOP — PACKAGING AND RETENTION

Updated: 2026-09-24
Status: **working hypotheses; audience research and script architecture still required**

## Recommended working package

**Title:** Why a 74%-Complete Nuclear Plant Was Still Too Expensive to Finish

**Thumbnail:** present-day Unit 3 cooling tower and unfinished complex, one dominant hero structure, a small progress graphic reading `74% BUILT`, no hazard symbol, no fake radiation glow, no abandoned-city collage.

Packaging promise: the video will use BPA's own 1994 comparison to explain why physical progress did not equal economic value.

Accuracy control: `74%` refers specifically to WNP-3 in BPA's 1994 record. The title and thumbnail must not visually imply both units were 74 percent complete.

## Title alternatives

1. **They Built 74% of a Nuclear Plant — Then the Math Collapsed**
2. **Why Two Nuclear Projects Died 12 Years Apart**
3. **The $2.6 Billion Nuclear Project That Never Made Power**
4. **The Nuclear Plant That Became an Industrial Park**

Controls:

- `$2.6 billion` is WNP-3 investment expressed in 1993 dollars in BPA's 1994 record.
- Do not use `$12 billion` as the cost of Satsop; GAO used it for projected completion of Projects 4 and 5 together.
- Do not use the $2.25 billion bond figure as WNP-3 spending.

## Cold-open promise

Visual sequence:

1. a present-day tower with no context;
2. a sourced plan separating Unit 3 from Unit 5;
3. `WNP-5 — terminated 1982`;
4. `WNP-3 — suspended 1983 / terminated 1994`;
5. the question: why did neighboring reactors die twelve years apart?

The opening should reveal the dual timeline within the first 60–90 seconds. Do not delay the core distinction for generic nuclear history.

## Provisional long-form retention architecture

1. **Cold open — The wrong assumption:** it looks like one abandoned plant.
2. **The forecast:** why the Northwest thought it needed a nuclear fleet.
3. **The machine grows:** WPPSS scales from a small agency into a five-project builder.
4. **Split the site:** explain WNP-3 and WNP-5 physically and financially.
5. **First collapse:** Projects 4 and 5 terminate; bond obligations remain.
6. **Midpoint reversal:** WNP-3 survives, already roughly three-quarters built.
7. **The 1994 decision:** sunk cost versus the cost and risk of completion.
8. **Concrete after purpose:** permit closure, asset sales, public transfer.
9. **Second life:** business park, manufacturing, utilities, training.
10. **Callback:** the towers are not monuments to one decision; they are a cross-section of how megaprojects outlive their forecasts.

## Anti-repetition rules

- No continuous “reconstruction” bug across the whole film.
- No repeated Ken Burns push on every still.
- Use motion only when it carries information: map movement, construction progress, financing flow, schedule, or spatial explanation.
- Alternate archive, document, GFX, present-day footage, and restrained reconstruction by narrative function.
- Hold architectural shots steady long enough to establish scale; avoid artificial micro-zoom or per-image shake.
- Reserve the most complete tower/reaction-building reveal for the 74-percent midpoint.
