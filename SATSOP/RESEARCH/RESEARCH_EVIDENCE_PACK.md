## 1. What the viewer is looking at

The unfinished structures at Satsop belong to **Washington Nuclear Projects 3 and 5**, two of five nuclear projects pursued by the Washington Public Power Supply System (WPPSS, later Energy Northwest).

The visible site can easily suggest one failed twin-reactor plant. That shorthand is editorially dangerous. The two Satsop projects had materially different financing arrangements and different endings.

## 2. The regional premise

BPA's 1994 record says WPPSS undertook five nuclear projects in the 1970s to meet the Northwest electricity loads then projected for the 1980s and beyond. GAO documented the organizational strain: WPPSS had fewer than 100 staff before 1968, reached 1,471 employees by March 1979, and was projected to reach 2,400 by 1985.

This supports a systems story about demand forecasts, institutional expansion, financing capacity, and construction risk. It does not by itself prove corruption, fraud, or technical incompetence.

## 3. WNP-3

BPA's 1994 Record of Decision identifies WNP-3 as:

- near Satsop, 16 miles east of Aberdeen in Grays Harbor County;
- a planned **1,240 MW** pressurized-water reactor;
- based on Combustion Engineering's **System 80** design;
- equipped with a Westinghouse turbine generator.

Washington legislative material records that construction was suspended in **July 1983**. BPA later described the project as **74 percent complete**, with approximately **$2.6 billion invested in 1993 dollars**.

WNP-3 was preserved after suspension because future completion remained an option. Its story therefore continued for more than a decade after work stopped.

## 4. WNP-5

Contemporaneous GAO material identifies WNP-5 as a **1,240 MW** project under construction near Satsop. GAO reported it at about 8 percent complete in January 1980 and approximately 14 percent complete when Projects 4 and 5 were terminated.

Projects 4 and 5 were terminated on **January 22, 1982**, after financial advisers concluded that markets could not absorb the bond financing required for all five WPPSS projects. At termination, WPPSS estimated roughly $12 billion to complete Projects 4 and 5 together, about $8.5 billion above the original combined estimate. These are combined-project figures and must never be assigned to WNP-5 alone.

## 5. The financing split is the story engine

WNP-1 and 70 percent of WNP-3 were financed through revenue bonds whose costs were net-billed through BPA. Four investor-owned utilities held the other 30 percent of WNP-3; BPA later paid all WNP-3 project costs under the settlement structure described in its record.

Projects 4 and 5 used participating utilities and municipal tax-exempt bonds backed by “take or pay” contracts. GAO described those contracts as requiring payment whether or not the projects were completed, operable, or operating.

The **$2.25 billion bond figure** belongs to Projects 4 and 5 together. The subsequent default belongs to those bonds. It must not be attached to WNP-3 merely because WNP-3 shared the Satsop site with WNP-5.

## 6. Why 74 percent complete was still not enough

In its May 1994 decision, BPA compared WNP-3 completion scenarios with a combined-cycle combustion-turbine alternative. BPA found combined cycle cheaper than its base and high WNP-3 cases; the low WNP-3 case was only comparable on a life-cycle basis.

BPA concluded that the possible benefit did not justify WNP-3's capital exposure and other business risks. The record cites an additional capital investment at risk, potential decommissioning and waste-disposal costs, and regulatory/operating uncertainties.

This is the episode's strongest counterintuitive fact: a project's physical completion percentage does not determine whether finishing it is economically rational.

## 7. Termination and transfer

The decision not to complete the suspended Satsop reactors was made in 1994. BPA's later official financial disclosure records Project 3 as terminated in June 1994. NRC records identify the WNP-3 construction permit as revoked in January 1999.

The later BPA disclosure records:

- formation of the Satsop Redevelopment Project in 1995 by local public agencies;
- transfer of Project 3 site properties and facilities for economic development in 1999;
- local assumption of required site-remediation responsibility.

Exact distinctions among economic termination, state site certification, property transfer, and NRC permit revocation must remain explicit in the eventual chronology.

## 8. What the site became

The site is now Satsop Business Park, a Port of Grays Harbor facility. Current official material describes a mixed-use industrial park with large buildings, developed land, electrical service, industrial water, wastewater systems, gas, and fiber connectivity.

The Port describes:

- the 300,000-square-foot turbine building as repurposed for industrial/manufacturing use;
- actual underground tunnels used for training;
- the reactor building as a multi-level training environment;
- Cooling Tower 5 as a structure with a present use discussed in the Port's official tour.

The ruins-to-reuse ending is stronger than a generic “abandoned place” ending because it shows how sunk infrastructure can retain value even after its original purpose disappears.

## 9. What the film must not imply

- Neither Satsop reactor generated commercial electricity.
- Satsop is not a nuclear-accident story.
- “WPPSS default” is not sufficient explanation for WNP-3.
- “The reactors were 75 percent complete” is false as a plural claim.
- Cooling towers are heat-rejection structures; they are not visual proof of nuclear operation or contamination.
- Current business-park activity must not be erased to make the site feel more abandoned.

## 11. Provisional thesis

**Satsop is a film about the gap between physical completion and economic completion.**

The towers survived because industrial megaprojects do not vanish when a spreadsheet changes. They leave contracts, debt, reinforced concrete, transmission infrastructure, local expectations, and a second life that no original forecast imagined.
