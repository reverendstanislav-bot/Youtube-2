## Working premise (English US)
In 1965, NASA built two machines so heavy they needed a specially engineered road just to carry a rocket to the pad. Six decades later, with three generations of spaceflight behind them, one of those machines still performs that job. Why was it built, how does the thing move without breaking a rocket or sinking its road, and what had to change to survive Artemis?

Proposed audience promise: reveal the engineering under the rocket — not a generic Apollo history and NOT a montage of Saturn V launches.

Working video-title A: "NASA Built a 6.6-Million-Pound Machine in 1965. It Still Works." (NASA построило машину весом 6,6 миллиона фунтов в 1965 году. Она работает до сих пор.)
Working video-title B: "How NASA Moves an 18-Million-Pound Load at 1 MPH" (Как NASA перемещает груз в 18 миллионов фунтов со скоростью 1 миля в час.)
Working video-title C: "The Giant Machine That Apollo Left Behind — And NASA Still Needs" (Гигантская машина, оставшаяся после Apollo, которая всё ещё нужна NASA.)
Titles proposed only, no title lock. 18M lb means maximum upgraded payload rating, not payload in every individual rollout.

## Evidence anchors
E01 — NASA identifies two crawler transporters constructed in 1965 to move the Saturn V between VAB and Launch Complex 39. NASA's chronology says first crawler motion Jan 23 1965 and first Saturn V to pad Aug 26 1967.
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/exploration-ground-systems/the-crawlers/
STATUS Supported.

E02 — NASA / 2023 Guinness officially cites crawler-transporter 2 as the heaviest self-powered vehicle at about 6.65 million pounds; this is crawler weight itself, not crawler+rocket.
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/nasas-rocket-transporter-crawls-into-history-books-with-world-record/
STATUS Supported, rounded from article.

E03 — NASA official crawler page lists upgraded CT-2 load capacity 18 million pounds; do not claim that every rocket assembly weighs 18 million pounds.
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/exploration-ground-systems/the-crawlers/
STATUS Supported with strict payload-vs-vehicle distinction.

E04 — NASA says 4.2-mile VAB-to-pad trip can take eight to 12 hours, about one mph nominal. Do not assert a fixed universal voyage time.
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/nasas-rocket-transporter-crawls-into-history-books-with-world-record/
STATUS Supported range, not a single speed/duration.

E05 — NASA describes locomotive/diesel-electric power, specialized rock/gravel crawlerway, refurbishment for heavier Artemis equipment and staff monitoring; upgrades are essential. Avoid "unchanged since 1965".
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/curious-universe/artemis-ii-the-ground-teams-powering-nasas-moon-mission/
STATUS Supported at overview level; engineering claims require NASA technical records at Stage04.

E06 — After Apollo and Skylab, same crawler type was used for Space Shuttle launch rollouts, then CT-2 refurbished for Artemis. Do not claim a single serial-number vehicle moved ALL launches without per-vehicle logs.
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/exploration-ground-systems/the-crawlers/
STATUS Supported with serial-ID caveat.

E07 — NASA article documents CT-2 movement of Artemis II rocket before launch in January 2026. NASA also documents February 25 2026 rollback, demonstrating current real machine operation. Do not use a 2026 article's future tense as current October 2026 status.
SOURCE https://www.nasa.gov/blogs/missions/2026/02/25/nasa-artemis-ii-rocket-rolls-back-to-vehicle-assembly-building/
STATUS Supported for Feb 2026 action.

E08 — NASA official NASA Archives + NARA Apollo series list authentic motion film including crawler in Apollo sequences, available with official access and HD surrogates (catalog listings not physically downloaded or shot-screened).
SOURCE https://www.archives.gov/research/motion-pictures/apollo-av
SOURCE https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Crawler-Transporter_footage_(KSC-19640101-MH-NAS01-0001-The_Crawler_Transporter_The_Beginning_Historical_Footage-B_2309).webm
STATUS Lead verified as catalog records only.
