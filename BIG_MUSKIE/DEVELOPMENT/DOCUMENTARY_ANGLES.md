# BIG MUSKIE — DOCUMENTARY ANGLE DEVELOPMENT

These are development candidates, not rankings locked by the user.

## Angle A — The machine built for one economic/geologic problem

**Core question:** What problem could justify building a 27-million-pound machine — and what changed so completely that it was scrapped only three decades later?

- Hook strength: very high
- Scale: very high
- Mystery: high
- Retention: very high
- Visual availability: high
- Archive availability: high but rights work required
- 15–30 min sustainability: excellent
- Shorts: excellent

Story strength: joins geology, mining economics, engineering, operations, regulation/market change, and the surviving bucket. Avoids becoming a specification list.

## Angle B — The giant electric machine inside a coal-power system

**Core question:** Why did an electrically powered machine of this scale exist to expose coal feeding the regional power economy?

- Hook strength: high
- Scale: very high
- Mystery: high
- Retention: high
- Visual availability: high
- Archive availability: medium-high
- 15–30 min sustainability: excellent
- Shorts: strong

Story strength: reveals Big Muskie as infrastructure inside infrastructure, not a standalone monster machine.

## Angle C — How a 27-million-pound machine walked and dug

**Core question:** How do you move, rotate, power, and operate a machine whose bucket alone weighs hundreds of tons?

- Hook strength: very high
- Scale: very high
- Mystery: medium-high
- Retention: high if intercut with story
- Visual availability: very high for diagrams/reconstructions
- Archive availability: medium
- 15–30 min sustainability: good, but risks becoming engineering encyclopedia
- Shorts: excellent

Best used as the engineering core inside Angle A rather than the whole film.

## Angle D — The machine that reshaped the land, and the land after the machine

**Core question:** What did mining at this scale do to southeastern Ohio, and what became of the landscape after Big Muskie disappeared?

- Hook strength: high
- Scale: high
- Mystery: high
- Retention: high
- Visual availability: high
- Archive availability: medium-high
- 15–30 min sustainability: strong
- Shorts: strong

Needs evidence-balanced treatment: industrial need, disturbance, reclamation, conservation, and community memory without simplistic advocacy.

## Angle E — Why gigantism lost

**Core question:** Big Muskie represented a bet that one unprecedented machine could beat the economics of overburden. Why did smaller/more efficient systems ultimately win?

- Hook strength: high
- Scale: very high
- Mystery: very high
- Retention: very high
- Visual availability: medium-high
- Archive availability: medium
- 15–30 min sustainability: strong
- Shorts: excellent

Needs more primary evidence on maintenance, operating cost, availability, and replacement technology before it can lead the final script.

## Development recommendation

Use **Angle A as the spine**, with:
- Angle C for the engineering reveal;
- Angle B for the surrounding industrial system;
- Angle D for consequences/ending;
- Angle E as a carefully sourced late-film economic interpretation.

This recommendation is a working development hypothesis and requires user approval before script lock.
