## Cold open — sourced working draft

Today, almost nothing is left — just one enormous steel bucket in southeastern Ohio.

That bucket belonged to a machine that weighed more than twenty-seven million pounds. Its job was not to mine coal directly. It had to remove the earth above the coal, hundreds of tons at a time, so an entire mining and power system could reach the seam below.

Big Muskie went to work in 1969. In January 1991, it stopped.

Eight years later, the machine itself was cut apart for scrap.

So the real story is not simply how America built something this large. It is why the geology and economics of one Ohio mine made a machine this extreme seem rational — and why that logic eventually disappeared.

### Cold-open rule

Do not reveal every shutdown cause in the first 20 seconds. The open promises the explanation and uses the surviving bucket as the mystery object.

## Story promise

By the end of the film, the viewer should understand:
- what problem Big Muskie was built to solve;
- why a dragline was selected;
- how the bucket, boom, tub, motors, and walking system actually worked;
- what infrastructure/crew/terrain a machine of this scale required;
- what scale bought economically and what it cost operationally;
- why it stopped in 1991;
- why preservation failed in 1999;
- why the bucket and reclaimed landscape are the most revealing remains.

## Retention architecture / chapter spine

### 0 — The thing that survived
Present-day bucket. Do not show the entire answer immediately. Question: what machine needed this?

### 1 — The problem beneath Ohio
Deep overburden, coal seam, hilly mine property, growing power demand. Establish why ordinary stripping capacity was not enough.

### 2 — Build it where it will work
Bucyrus-Erie, 1966 project start, rail/truck logistics, onsite assembly, 1969 start. Reveal full machine scale.

### 3 — How Big Muskie actually worked
Dragline cycle, 220 yd³ bucket, boom/tub, motors, swing, walking mechanism. Use changing media: archive → technical drawing → diagram → human-scale archive.

### 4 — The machine around the machine
Crews, maintenance, cable/power, haul/coal handling, mine geometry, road width/grade, power-system connection. Question: what does it take to keep this working?

### 5 — What scale bought
Production/output, overburden removed, the economic logic of one huge stripper. Introduce the downside: availability, terrain, maintenance, specialization.

### 6 — The logic changes
More efficient mining technology + weaker high-sulfur-coal demand after 1990 Clean Air Act changes. Big Muskie idled January 1991.

### 7 — Saving the unsaveable
Preservation efforts, liability/reclamation constraints, 1999 dismantling. Delay bucket-survival payoff until this chapter.

### 8 — After Big Muskie
Miners' Memorial Park, bucket, reclaimed landscape, what remains of the industrial system and its human history.

## Pacing safeguards
