# BIG MUSKIE — STAGE 5 PRODUCTION LOCK

Status: **PRODUCTION DEVELOPMENT LOCK — 2026-09-22**

This is not a publication lock. It is the canonical creative/evidence direction for script production.

## Locked documentary angle

**Scale with a reason.**

Core question:

> What problem could make a 27-million-pound machine economically rational — and what changed so completely that the machine was scrapped three decades later?

The film is not built around "look how huge it was." Scale is introduced only when it answers a narrative question.

## Story thesis

Big Muskie was not an isolated stunt. It was one component of a much larger industrial system: deep overburden, a huge Ohio surface mine, growing electric-power demand, and a mine-expansion program designed to move enormous quantities of earth efficiently.

Its disappearance is explained by the collapse of that specific operating logic: changing mining technology, changing economics for high-sulfur Ohio coal, reclamation obligations, and the difficulty of preserving a machine this large.

## Locked story promise

By the end, the viewer understands:

1. why Central Ohio Coal needed this much stripping capacity;
2. how a 220-yd³ dragline actually worked;
3. how it moved, swung and powered its major motions;
4. what infrastructure and crews were required around it;
5. what industrial scale bought;
6. what the 1977 operating data reveals about the reality behind the spectacle;
7. why the economics changed;
8. why the whole machine was not preserved;
9. what survived physically and in the reclaimed landscape.
