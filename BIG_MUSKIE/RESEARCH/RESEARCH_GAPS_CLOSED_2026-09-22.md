## Newly found direct / near-primary evidence

### 1969 — World's Largest Dragline
Melvin W. Kraschnewski, SME/AIME.

The abstract explicitly identifies:
- the 1966 Central Ohio Coal Company ↔ Bucyrus-Erie contract;
- a 220-cubic-yard dragline;
- its purpose: stripping overburden near Cumberland, Ohio;
- application sizing based on annual coal output, overburden depth and terrain;
- BECO designation **4250-W**;
- the paper as a design/construction treatment.

Kraschnewski is also named on 1968 U.S. Patent 3,531,088 as an inventor assigning large-dragline hoist technology to Bucyrus-Erie Company.

### 1969 — Central Ohio Coal Company's Mine Expansion Program
Paul D. Martinka, AIME.

Documents the broader economic system:
- Muskingum mine supplied AEP/Ohio Power generation;
- Muskingum River Unit 5 added 615 MW in 1968;
- plant coal requirement rose from 2.6m to 4.4m tons/year;
- Central Ohio Coal undertook a **$41m expansion/modernization** involving the world's largest dragline and an electric railroad.

This directly supports the film thesis that Big Muskie was not a stunt; it was part of a power-generation expansion system.

### 1969 — Big Muskie: King of the Giants
Coal Age 74(12):50–61.

EPA's bibliography preserves the contemporary abstract:
- 220-yd³ bites;
- designed to remove overburden greater than 100 ft;
- mining machinery and coal handling described as a complete system;
- large equipment linked to reclamation-cost reduction.

### 1977 — U.S. Bureau of Mines-sponsored Penn State field study
`Application of a Total System Surface Mine Simulator to Coal Stripping, Volume VI`.

Actual Muskingum / BE4250-W data include:
- measured mean cycle **75.25 s** at ~120° swing;
- actual-study-month mechanical availability **35%**;
- actual-data-row dragline overburden **1,949,200 yd³/month**.

Scope warning: these are values for the study dataset/month, not lifetime averages.
