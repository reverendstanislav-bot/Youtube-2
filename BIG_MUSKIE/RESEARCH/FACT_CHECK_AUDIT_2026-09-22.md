Status: **COMPLETE FOR STAGE 0–4. Corrections applied to canonical research files.**

## Method

Every important claim from the initial evidence pack was cross-checked against the strongest available source classes:
1. current ODNR historical markers / Ohio geological material;
2. AEP primary announcements;
3. contemporary Bucyrus-Erie publication material;
4. Caterpillar successor-company history;
5. EPA / U.S. National Archives;
6. HCEA specialist archive;
7. reputable secondary reporting for oral history only.

Where sources conflict, the audit does **not** average them or select the larger/more dramatic figure.

## Source-specific problems found

### Caterpillar
Strong overview, but its 330-ft boom conflicts with current ODNR and Ohio geological sources giving 310 ft. Use Caterpillar for project/logistics/history, not as sole dimensional authority.

### AEP 2001 dedication release
It says Big Muskie moved "483 million tons" of material. AEP's earlier memorial release and current ODNR marker say **483 million cubic yards**. The 2001 unit is treated as an internal inconsistency/likely wording error.

### Guinness
Its page identifies the record category correctly, but its displayed lifetime volume is only 4.8 million cubic yards while comparing it to nearly twice Panama Canal excavation. That is irreconcilable with AEP/ODNR's >483 million yd³ and internally suspicious. Guinness is downgraded for quantitative production claims.

### Tourism pages
Useful for current visitor context, not for resolving engineering specs. Several derived numbers differ from primary/official material.

### WOSU
Excellent recent human/oral-history reporting. Statements about day-to-day operation/breakdowns are attributed recollections, not independently measured availability data.

## Final Stage 0–4 factual position

The central documentary concept survives the audit:
**Big Muskie was not a pointless giant. It was a highly specialized stripping machine built because the geology, scale, mine plan and power-economy of Central Ohio made extreme size economically rational for a period. Its disappearance is best explained by the collapse of that specific operating logic — changing mining technology, high-sulfur-coal economics, reclamation obligations and preservation constraints — not by a single sensational cause.**

This is strong enough to proceed to Stage 5, provided all final narration numbers follow the canonical wording in this audit.
