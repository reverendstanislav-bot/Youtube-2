## 10 viewer perspectives

1. Casual YouTube viewer
   - The cold open is clear and immediately establishes scale.
   - The story remains understandable, but the beige desert / machine / wheel visual language becomes familiar by the middle.
   - The ending is the weakest part: the centered channel slogan feels like a placeholder rather than a premium documentary sign-off.

2. Industrial-history enthusiast
   - Archive documents, patents, Yuma material, and survivor imagery are the strongest trust-building beats.
   - Reconstructions are useful, but the film would benefit from keeping documentary evidence visually dominant whenever possible.
   - The final CTA should feel archival/editorial, not like a generic channel bumper.

3. Engineer / mechanism-focused viewer
   - Electric Wheel is substantially clearer after V6.
   - Mechanical progression is strong, but a few long static technical holds still feel presentation-like.
   - The end card does not visually connect to the engineering language established in the film.

4. Documentary / cinema viewer
   - Overall grade is coherent, but generated desert reconstructions share a similar beige/olive photographic family.
   - The final 14.5-second plate is too static and typographically centered, creating a dead visual stop instead of a controlled resolution.

5. AI-skeptical viewer
   - ARCHIVE / DOCUMENT / RECONSTRUCTION / CONCEPT grammar is essential and generally works.
   - Some generated shots remain polished enough that the label has to do a lot of trust work.
   - The branded end plate should not imitate archival evidence; it should clearly read as modern editorial design.

6. Mobile viewer
   - Main subtitles are readable.
   - Small provenance labels are less immediately legible on a phone.
   - The final centered slogan competes with subtitles and creates too many text layers in the same visual zone.

7. Sound-sensitive viewer
   - VO remains clear and the V5/V6 mix is technically controlled.
   - The main music cue architecture becomes very sparse at the end; the current original music bed stops at about 19:45, leaving roughly the last 51 seconds without a musical resolve.
   - This makes the closing section feel flatter and more somber than the visuals require.

8. Retention editor
   - Opening retention grammar is much stronger than earlier versions.
   - The main remaining fatigue risk is not shot count but similarity: repeated machine/desert/reconstruction families make some middle stretches feel longer than their actual edit cadence.
   - The final CTA should be shorter-feeling through hierarchy and negative space, not more text.

9. Premium-documentary viewer
   - The final survivor / empty-range sequence is conceptually strong.
   - The current centered HIDDEN INDUSTRIAL AMERICA / THE MACHINE WORKED / THE WORLD MOVED ON plate looks like a temporary title card placed over an otherwise cinematic image.
   - A restrained asymmetrical end composition would feel materially more expensive.

10. Potential subscriber / returning viewer
   - The spoken CTA is sufficient.
   - The baked end screen does not need to repeat the sales message; it needs to establish a recognizable series identity and leave room for YouTube end-screen elements.
   - The right side of the frame should remain visually quiet for platform overlays.

## Cross-viewer conclusion

The film is materially stronger than V5, but FULL_V6 should not be approved as final yet.
