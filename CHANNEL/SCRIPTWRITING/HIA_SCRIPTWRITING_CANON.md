## One investigation

Define one central question about an American industrial problem, machine, network or consequential decision. Introduce it in the opening; answer it substantially in the final major act. Every chapter changes understanding, rather than adding unrelated facts.

Preferred engine: IMPOSSIBLE PROBLEM → UNEXPECTED SOLUTION → ESCALATING OBSTACLES → HIDDEN CONSEQUENCE → FINAL REVELATION.

These are functions, not invented historical events. A real constraint need not literally be impossible. Success, adaptation, economic displacement and lasting transformation are valid outcomes. Never manufacture catastrophe, secrecy, villains or a third breakthrough. Do not hide a famous outcome merely to simulate suspense: investigate why/how it happened. Avoid chronological encyclopedia summaries.

## Flexible architecture

| Act | Position guide | Narrative function |
|---|---|---|
| 01 Cold open | 0–5% | Verified anomaly, contradiction, failed task or challenge |
| 02 Context and surprise | 5–15% | Relevant origin, constraint or unexpected scale |
| 03 Turn 1 | 15–35% | First problem, decision, mechanism and result |
| 04 Turn 2 | 35–55% | Consequence creates a harder/different problem |
| 05 Turn 3 | 55–77% | Decisive limitation, discovery or changed mission |
| 06 Main payoff | 77–87% | Explain the opening paradox with evidence |
| 07 Reassessment | 87–98% | Legacy, consequences and known/unknown boundary |
| 08 Final CTA | 98–100% | Specific question and natural subscription opportunity |

Three revelations are preferred, not mandatory. They may be decisions, construction stages, compromises, failures, attempts or events. Merge/split/rename acts when evidence supports a stronger causal story; record why. Proportions are guides, not timestamps or word quotas. Do not repeat a payoff to fill two headings. A consequence can itself be the major revelation.

## First minute: highest editorial priority

Design roughly 0–15 seconds for immediate hook, 15–30 for stakes, 30–45 for paradox, 45–60 for question and promise. These are flexible design targets, not measured timing or algorithm laws. Keep a strong question earlier if it belongs earlier.

By the opening's end viewers know what happened, why it matters, what remains unexplained and what continuing will reveal. Use verified scale/risk/cost only with units/context, not a specification dump. Do not explain away the mystery or list future repairs. No subscription CTA or generic channel/history introduction in the cold open.

## Engineering revelation sequence

1. Problem: physical, economic, geographical or operational constraint in plain language.
2. Obvious approach: baseline and why insufficient. Do not claim engineers actually considered/rejected it without evidence; identify analytical comparison as explanation.
3. Unexpected decision: actual choice, attributed to the correct people/organizations.
4. Mechanism: how parts, forces, layouts or workflows interact; source-grounded explanation, not praise for genius.
5. Result: distinguish designed, demonstrated and commercial performance, then historical significance. A successful test is not an operational fleet or profitable business.
6. Hidden cost/new question: the consequence motivating the next chapter. If there is no hidden cost, use a real next constraint rather than invent one.

Each major chapter delivers a new answer and a consequential new question. Emotion arises from documented actions, effort, risk, loss or changed purpose.

## Retention and language

Maintain one main open loop; resolve smaller loops and let consequences lead onward. Escalate difficulty, scale, cost, consequence or changed expectations; not every chapter needs a larger machine. Roughly every 45–75 seconds review meaningful information progression, not compulsory cliffhangers/cuts. Use [retention QC](HIA_RETENTION_QC.md).

Write natural spoken American English for US adults interested in engineering, infrastructure and industrial history. Use strong verbs, concrete nouns, varied sentences and accessible mechanisms. Questions need consequential answers. Remove repeated theses/specifications, unnecessary background, empty superlatives and recycled suspense formulas. No invented dialogue, thoughts, motives or unsupported first/largest/secret claims.

If RU review is required, preserve stable paragraph IDs and equivalent facts, caveats and CTAs. Separate narration from source/editor/pronunciation notes. TTS cleanup must not silently change locked text.
