# PROJECT PLUTO — FACT CONTROL V1

Status: STAGE 01 FIRST PASS; not final fact/legal PASS.

## SAFE WITH SOURCE CITATION
- Project Pluto was a US nuclear ramjet program associated with the proposed SLAM missile (LLNL history P02; original engineering reports P04/P05).
- There were ground tests of Tory II-A in 1961 and Tory II-C in 1964 at Nevada Test Site (P01/P02).
- Tory II-C achieved a successful May 20, 1964 full-power ground test under simulated flight conditions (P01).
- July 1, 1964 is the documented date DOD notified AEC not to pursue flight testing; the program was phased out (P01).
- No SLAM flight tests were performed; the ground-tested engine is not an operational SLAM missile (P01/P11).
- Nevada Pluto Facility is distinct from other nuclear rocket/space propulsion facilities at the site (LOC P07/P08).

## USE WITH PRECISE QUALIFICATION
- 500 MW is LLNL's rounded thermal power scale; do not title-lock or imply mechanical thrust magnitude (P02).
- ICBMs' development contributed to changing strategic rationale, according to later historical sources; primary 1964 decision states no flight objective, not one definitive motive (P01/P10/P11).
- Possible effects of the missile's exhaust, acoustic signature, flight profile and intended payload: historical design/projections only, never actual tested flight consequences.
- “Successful test” refers to defined engine conditions, not proof that a complete missile would meet range, safety, control or reliability targets.
- “World's first” and “unlimited range” require scoped independent sourcing.
