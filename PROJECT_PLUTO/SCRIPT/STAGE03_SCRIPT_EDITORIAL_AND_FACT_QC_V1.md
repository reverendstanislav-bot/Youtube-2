## Primary source provenance, independently examined
**P01 — official 1964 AEC chronology:** *U.S. Aeronautics and Space Activities 1964*, AEC Chapter V, **printed page 63 / PDF indexed page 66**:
https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/presrep1964.pdf
Verified exact underlying record (not a historian's speculation):
- test facility air blowdowns resumed Jan 1964; Tory II-C reactor shipped mid-Feb;
- first criticality March 25 in test vehicle;
- May 12 intermediate level, simulated Mach 2.8 at 10,000-ft;
- May 20 initial full power, simulated sea-level Mach 2.8;
- both called unqualifiedly successful in official 1964 federal report;
- DOD notified AEC **July 1** that flight-test objective would not be pursued; planned reactor tests cancelled and program/facilities phased out / mothballed.
**P02 — Lawrence Livermore Laboratory 65th anniversary, 1955 Nuclear Propulsion section:** https://www.llnl.gov/sites/www/files/llnl_65th_anniversary_book.pdf
Authoritative institutional history confirms Livermore shift into Pluto in 1957, Tory II-A May 14 1961 45-sec test, approximate 500 MW **thermal reactor scale** of II-C and materials/air-handling/remote work. Retrospective nature labeled; not an independent contemporaneous trial log.
**P04 — contemporary Tory II-C study, UCRL-7036:** https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4332363
Identifies Tory II-A as feasibility and Tory II-C as intended ramjet engine, distinguishes additional demanding integrated reactor controls; not evidence flying SLAM existed.
**P05 — contemporary Tory II-A controls, UCRL-6590 (1961):** https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/4829009
Stored air at test facility limited Tory II-A design-point operation to less than one minute; **do not extrapolate** to endurance of Tory II-C or proposed airborne SLAM.
