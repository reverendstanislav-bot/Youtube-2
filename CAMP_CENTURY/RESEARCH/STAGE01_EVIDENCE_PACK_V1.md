## Core documentary
The U.S. Army built Camp Century beneath the Greenland ice sheet from 1959 to 1960. The camp included housing, work spaces and a portable power plant. The remarkable logistical and construction story is documented in period Army film. By 1967 operations ended; the base's tunnels deformed as snow and firn moved. An associated larger military concept, Project Iceworm, was proposed but not deployed.

## Facts validated from sources
1. Started 1959, constructed through October 1960; up to ~200 people: https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/18/4029/2024/
2. Cut-and-cover trench construction and deformation roughly 0.1–1.0 m annually at documented sections: same 2024 study.
3. Larger proposed Project Iceworm was never implemented; do not claim missiles were deployed: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/2016GL069688
4. Reactor dismantled and removed in 1964; scientific and physical site infrastructure remained: https://www.usace.army.mil/About/History/Exhibits/Nuclear-Power-Program/Experimental/
5. Seasonal presence ended by 1967; do not portray abrupt one-night collapse: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2021.578978/full
6. Modern NASA radar shows remnants buried at least 30 m: https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/new-view-of-the-city-under-the-ice-153616/
7. Historic ice cores and underlying sediment retain scientific value: https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/18/4029/2024/
