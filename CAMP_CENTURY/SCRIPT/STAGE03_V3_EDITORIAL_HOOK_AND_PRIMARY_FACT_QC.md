## Original report corrections (important: supersedes mistaken V2 QC)
**The prior assistant claimed “12% cross-sectional area loss per year is unsupported.” THAT STATEMENT WAS INCORRECT.** The U.S. Army's ORIGINAL *Camp Century: Evolution of Concept and History of Design, Construction and Performance*, CRREL TR174, October 1965 includes:
- Figure 28, **printed p39 / PDF P46 (0-indexed)**: `Reduction in cross sectional area = 12% per year` for one specific **Main Trench station 6+00**.
- Figure 29, same page: `17% per year` for **Trench 20 station 2+60**.
- Table II, **printed p37–38 / PDF P44–45**: average annual measured height closure `16 in/year` for Main Trench; `34 in/year` for side trenches. These are **vertical closure rates**, not area-reduction percentages and not universal rates across all places.
- **printed p41 / PDF P48**: based on measured deformation, anticipated need for **~20,000 cubic yards to be trimmed and removed per year** to maintain original cross-sections. A projection, not documentary proof this volume was actually removed every year.
- **printed p43–44 / PDF P50–51**: heated liquid waste spread through firn; snow floor near the sump settled roughly **four feet**, prompting removal of two nearby T-5 buildings. This is *local sump consequence*, not the entire underground city dropping suddenly.
- **printed p31 / PDF P38**: historical Camp Century total cost **$7,920,000**, with **$5,700,000 allocated to PM-2A nuclear power plant**. Historic dollars; no equivalent current dollar claim.
- **printed p32 / PDF P39**: transport from Camp Tuto about **138 miles**; approx **1,000 short tons moved in 1959** and **4,600 in 1960**. Combined 5,600 short tons is an arithmetic total of these reported seasonal shipments, not independent verified finished-camp weight.
- **printed pp47–51 / PDF P54–58**: PM-2A physical power station operating performance, shutdown 1963 and removal 1964; no live reactor remains buried.

## Higher-level evidence and qualifying limitations
- Nielsen, Nielsen & Martin-Nielsen, *City Under the Ice* (2014), historical research p445, https://pure.au.dk/ws/files/81615219/City_under_the_Ice_2014.pdf: proposed Iceworm **600 missiles** and **2100 potential launch sites**; contextualizes both Cold War rival service programs and Danish politics. This is **not an actual missile deployment**.
- Colgan et al. 2016 peer-reviewed study https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/2016GL069688/full: 1962 proposed Iceworm; 1963 project rejection, year-round operations reduced 1964, seasonal shutdown by 1967; later physically abandoned materials and emissions-conditional concern.
