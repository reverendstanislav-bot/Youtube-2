## High-signal comparison

1. **The core story has proven audience interest.** Many versions have attracted substantial views; a new channel is not guaranteed any views merely because the topic works.
2. **Broad cold-audience titles avoid assuming familiarity with "Lake Peigneur".** The best-performing browse-oriented formulations prominently contain "entire lake", "vanished", "drained", or a concrete physical impossibility.
3. **Big-channel and small-channel performance differ.** Plainly Difficult has an established 1.1M base and a descriptive history title; copying that packaging onto a channel with zero return viewers is risky.
4. **Abismo Marinho is the most relevant counterexample to the no-audience hypothesis:** ~337K views on a channel listed at ~3K followers. This suggests a small channel *can* get distribution, not that every newcomer will.
5. **Exact-title copying demonstrably does not transfer audience:** Dark Records ~6.69M and Real World ~24K used the SAME title.
6. **Avoid misleading causality.** "The wrong survey/coordinates definitely caused the breach" is stronger than the project's MSHA-backed fact control. A precise technical initiating error was not conclusively established.
7. **Avoid misinformation around mine headcount.** Use the project's source-locked "52 people underground", not indiscriminately "55 miners" or a mistaken claim that 52 died.
