On November 20, 1980, 52 people were underground in the Jefferson Island salt mine when water from Louisiana's Lake Peigneur began entering the workings. Above them, the drilling platform was in trouble — and the lake itself was about to change.

Within hours, the drilling platform was gone, part of the shoreline had collapsed, and the Delcambre Canal had begun flowing toward the lake instead of away from it. All 52 people recorded underground escaped. The seven workers on the drilling platform also got clear. No human lives were lost.

But the most repeated explanation of Lake Peigneur is cleaner than the surviving evidence. The federal mine-safety investigation documented the drilling operation, the mine and the inundation, but it did not conclusively establish the precise initiating technical error.

This episode of Hidden Industrial America follows the disaster from the first warning signs underground through the evacuation, the collapse at the surface, the reversed canal, the roughly 150-foot temporary waterfall, the lawsuits that followed, and the continuing industrial use of the Jefferson Island salt dome.
