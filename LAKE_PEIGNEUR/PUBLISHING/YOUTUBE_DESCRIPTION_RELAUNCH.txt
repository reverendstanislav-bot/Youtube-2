On November 20, 1980, water from Louisiana's Lake Peigneur found a path into the salt mine beneath it. The lake began draining underground, a drilling platform vanished, the shoreline collapsed, and the Delcambre Canal reversed direction.

Fifty-two people were recorded underground by the Mine Safety and Health Administration. All of them escaped. The seven workers on the drilling platform also got clear. No human lives were lost.

But the most repeated explanation of the disaster is cleaner than the evidence. The federal investigation documented the drilling operation, the flooded mine and an estimated connection near the mine workings — but it did not conclusively establish the precise initiating technical error.

This episode of Hidden Industrial America reconstructs the underground system, the evacuation, the collapse of the shoreline, the reversed canal, the roughly 150-foot temporary waterfall, the lawsuits that followed, and the industrial use of the Jefferson Island salt dome after the mine was destroyed.
