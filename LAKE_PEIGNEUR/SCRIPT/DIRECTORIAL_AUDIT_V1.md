## Verdict

**V1 = STRONG RESEARCH SCRIPT / NOT TTS-READY / REWRITE REQUIRED**

Overall directorial score: **76 / 100**

The core fix is structural:

> **Get the audience into the failure faster, let the evacuation and physical collapse carry the middle of the film, and move the full causation dispute until after the canal-reversal climax.**

## Critical directorial problems

### 1. The film explains the disputed cause before it has fully delivered the disaster

V1 §3 is accurate, but it stops the acceleration just as the audience is ready to see the system fail.

Current order:
- geology;
- mine depth;
- stuck drill;
- long uncertainty discussion;
- evacuation;
- collapse.

Better order:
- compressed geology;
- stuck drill / first water;
- evacuation;
- surface collapse;
- dissolution feedback;
- canal reversal;
- **then** investigation / disputed cause.

Reason:
Once the audience has seen what physically happened, they are ready to care about who was responsible and why the answer is not clean.

### 2. Act I has too much setup in two adjacent blocks

V1 §1 and §2 are both useful, but they function as one information package:
- salt dome;
- mine;
- oil;
- shallow lake;
- mine levels;
- room-and-pillar;
- salt dissolves.

They should become one compact visual explanation lasting roughly 75–100 seconds.

### 3. Dissolution is explained twice

V1 §2 explains the mechanism and then §6 explains it again.

Rewrite rule:
- Act I: give only the minimum rule — **fresh water dissolves salt**.
- Failure sequence: deliver the full feedback loop once, when the audience can see why it matters.

### 4. The evacuation needs to become a scene, not a summary

The strongest human fact is extraordinary:
**52 people underground; all out in less than an hour.**

V1 has the facts but spends too much time explaining what the sequence means.

Rewrite:
- water where it should not be;
- people distributed through workings;
- move toward exit;
- leadership/search actions;
- clock pressure;
- all 52 out.

Then explain why training mattered.

### 5. The first irreversible surface payoff needs to arrive earlier

The seven-man rig crew escaping and the platform disappearing should occur sooner.

That is the audience's first proof that the cold-open promise was real.

### 6. The canal reversal must remain the visual climax

Correct in V1.

Do not spend it in the opening.
Tease that "the canal would reverse" without showing/explaining the waterfall until the climax.

### 7. The causation section should become a forensic reveal

The strongest version is:

1. audience sees the mechanism;
2. audience assumes the answer to blame must be obvious;
3. film says: **it isn't**;
4. MSHA evidence / likely drill-hole location / competing expert positions;
5. distinction between mechanism and responsibility.

This is more satisfying than front-loading a disclaimer.

### 8. Legal aftermath should lose about one-third of its screen time

Keep:
- $260m original claim;
- $32m settlement;
- $12.8m Live Oak settlement;
- settlement is not causal proof;
- ~250 jobs lost.

Cut:
repetitive restatement that experts disagreed after the film has already made that point.

### 9. Modern gas-storage epilogue should be short

It is a strong HIA ending because it proves the geology remained industrially valuable.

But it should be a **30–45 second epilogue**, not a new chapter that feels like another film.

### 10. Ending should be shorter and harder

V1 ending has the right theme:
**infrastructure does not stop at the surface.**

V2 should land on that idea quickly, then end.
